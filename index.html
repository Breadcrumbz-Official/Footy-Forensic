1. Get a video

Telling us beats guessing. From a side-on camera the near and far leg overlap constantly, and picking the wrong one mirrors every result.

📐 Film side-on. Stand the camera level with the ball, square to the line of the shot, with the whole body in frame. Every fore/aft measurement — plant placement, backswing reach, torso lean — is read across the image, so a face-on angle flattens them to nothing and they will not be scored.

3. Results

What this system can and cannot measure
Reasonably detectable (2D landmarks, side-on camera)
  • Joint angles (knee, hip, ankle)
  • Torso lean relative to vertical
  • Limb positions relative to the hips, normalized by torso length
  • Whether the body mass is stacked over the support foot
  • Where the ball is, and the plant foot's fore/aft position relative to it
Unreliable / not measured
  • The exact point on the boot that met the ball (the ball is tracked as a circle, not a contact patch)
  • Ball speed, spin, or where the shot went
  • True 3D hip rotation from one camera — only a rough 2D proxy is shown
  • Lateral (side-to-side) plant-foot distance from the ball — only the fore/aft component is judged
  • Anything depending on depth toward/away from the camera

Metrics marked low confidence are shown but excluded from the score.
